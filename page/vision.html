When the copybook is a photograph

Plenty of copybooks are not files. They are printouts, or images inside a PDF nobody can select text out of, and the person who could retype them left in 2009.

Same model, same image, two
      attempts. One is right. The other names a field DALYTRANS-ID,
      truncates another to DALY, and parses as perfectly valid
      COBOL — nothing about its shape gives it away.

      
What settles it is that a copybook makes a
      falsifiable prediction. The field widths must sum to the record length printed on the
      page, and the real 105,000-byte data file must divide by it. A model read the paper;
      the arithmetic decided whether it read it right. An unreliable reader plus a decisive
      check is a reliable pipeline — so the tool simply re-reads until a layout is proved,
      and tells you how many attempts it took.
